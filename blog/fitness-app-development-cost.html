How Much Does It Cost to Develop a Fitness App in 2026?

The global fitness app market hit $15.2 billion in 2025 and continues to grow at 17% annually. But how much does it actually cost to build a fitness app that competes? The honest answer is: it depends — but we can give you real numbers.

Fitness App Types and Their Cost Ranges

Before estimating costs, you need to know which category your app falls into, as complexity varies enormously:

App Type Examples Estimated Cost
Simple workout tracker Step counter, basic logging $15,000–$40,000
Workout library app Video exercises, programs $40,000–$80,000
Personal trainer app Custom plans, trainer chat $70,000–$150,000
Full fitness platform Classes, nutrition, community $120,000–$300,000
AI-powered health coach Adaptive plans, biomarker analysis $200,000–$500,000+

Platform Choices and Their Impact on Budget

iOS Only

Most fitness apps generate higher revenue per user on iOS (higher LTV, stronger subscription conversion). Building iOS first is a common strategy for bootstrapped startups. Add approximately 15% to build watchOS companion app.

Android Only

Android dominates global market share outside the US/EU. If your target market is Southeast Asia, Africa, or Latin America, Android-first makes sense. Slightly more fragmentation testing required.

iOS + Android (React Native or Flutter)

Cross-platform frameworks reduce cost by 30–40% vs. native on both platforms. For fitness apps with standard UI patterns, React Native or Flutter deliver excellent performance. Reserve native development for apps requiring deep platform integration (advanced camera, AR fitness, complex sensor access).

Adding a Web App

A web dashboard for trainers and admins adds $20,000–$50,000. A full web application with workout features adds $40,000–$120,000.

Ongoing Costs After Launch

Development cost is just the beginning. Budget for these recurring expenses:

  • Cloud infrastructure: $500–$5,000/month (scales with users)
  • Video hosting/CDN: $300–$2,000/month for workout video streaming
  • App maintenance: 20% of initial development cost annually
  • New features: Continuous development to stay competitive
  • App Store fees: $99/year (Apple), $25 one-time (Google)
  • Third-party APIs: Wearable sync, nutrition database, payment processing

Ways to Reduce Fitness App Development Cost

  1. Build an MVP first: Launch with your top 3 features, learn what users actually want, then invest in expansion
  2. Use no-code/low-code for admin: Tools like Retool or AppSmith can replace custom-built admin dashboards at a fraction of the cost
  3. License content: Buying workout video libraries is cheaper than filming custom content
  4. Use white-label fitness platforms: A white-label fitness app can be customized and launched for 60–80% less than custom development
  5. Leverage third-party integrations: Apple Health, Google Fit, Strava — don't rebuild what exists

Conclusion

Fitness app development in 2026 ranges from $15,000 for a simple MVP to $500,000+ for a full-featured AI platform. The most important decision isn't your budget — it's figuring out the minimum viable product that validates your core value proposition. Build lean, launch fast, learn from real users, and invest in features that actually drive retention and revenue. The fitness apps winning today aren't the ones with the most features — they're the ones users actually come back to every day.
